1. Brand identity

The idea

Veleto is a premium notebook designed to turn scattered thoughts into structured ideas.

Some ideas arrive as sentences. Others arrive as a line, a question, or a feeling you cannot quite name. Veleto begins there. A quiet cover opens to a page with just enough structure to help you find your own. Dark color. A white line. A small daily ritual for making something of what is on your mind. Built around the notebook and its companion pen, Veleto gives unfinished thinking a place to become something.

Mission & audience

Make room for intentional thinking through considered notebooks and simple writing rituals.

Primary hypothesis: designers, independent founders, architects, writers, and creative professionals who already use notebooks for ideation and care about desk objects. Secondary: thoughtful gift buyers. Validate behavior and willingness to pay rather than assuming an age or income predicts demand.

When my ideas feel scattered, help me capture them and choose a next step in a space I want to return to.

Voice & visual direction

Quiet, specific, inviting, and confident. Use short sentences and concrete sensory language. Invite a practice; do not promise productivity, mental-health benefits, or genius. Say ‘A little room to think.’ Avoid ‘Unlock limitless potential.’ Never invent provenance, sustainability, scarcity, or customer praise.

Dark matte surfaces, tonal paper, generous negative space, restrained serif/sans contrast, and fine geometric white sketches. The notebooks remain blank outside. Use original artwork as example use, never as a promise of preprinted content. Product images are procedural concept renders, not physical samples.

Veleto creates considered notebooks designed to turn scattered thoughts into structured ideas. This request is for information, samples, and quotations only; it is not an order.

  • Colors: Black, Maroon, and Slate; request separate physical swatches.
  • Proposed closed dimensions: A5, 148 × 210 mm. Assumption pending founder approval; quote achievable dimensions and tolerances.
  • Proposed page count: 160 pages / 80 leaves. Assumption pending approval.
  • Proposed paper: dark, dyed-through, smooth uncoated paper; compare 120 gsm and 140 gsm. Paper type, weight, source, and white-ink performance require samples and approval.
  • Proposed cover: suitable rigid cover board with matte finish; exact material/finish pending supplier recommendation and founder approval.
  • Rounded corners on cover and page block; quote radius and tolerances.
  • Matching color ribbon bookmark; attachment, position, and width to be approved on sample.
  • Matching pen: opaque white ink, compatible with candidate paper, at least two tip sizes, replaceable/replenishable supply, barrel and finish options. Compatibility is unverified until tested.
  • Packaging: protect corners, keep pen from marking pages, quote plain and branded options separately. No environmental claims without documentation.
  • Branding: quote Veleto logo on cover/spine, ribbon tab, insert, and sleeve separately. Supply templates, proof/setup costs, print/foil/deboss choices, artwork ownership, and physical proofs. Do not produce branded samples before written approval.
  • Sample request: paper swatches, unbound sheets for pen tests, one bound notebook per color, candidate pen samples, and an assembled packaging sample. Quote sample cost, shipping, and lead time.
  • Quote an A5 closed format target, 148 × 210 mm; propose tolerances and achievable rounded-corner radius.
  • Quote 160 pages / 80 leaves in 120 and 140 gsm dyed-through stock. These are alternatives to sample, not fixed specifications. Assess thickness, opacity, drag, and binding together.
  • Provide separate Black, Maroon, and Slate paper and cover swatches. RGB/hex codes are digital references only; approve physical masters under daylight and warm indoor light.
  • Subtle 5 mm tonal dot grid; blank outside covers; no preprinted example sketches. Sample grid visibility against white ink.
  • Quote sewn, lay-flat binding and suitable alternatives; request construction cross-sections and a fully bound sample. No lay-flat claim until tested.
  • Matte cover material, rounded corners, matching ribbon, and color-coordinated page edges. Request rub and scuff tests; confirm material composition.
  • Source a reliable opaque white-ink pen and replacement supply. Test 0.5/0.8/1.0 mm ball options; ball diameter is not line width. Approve writing experience before approving barrel aesthetics.
  • Paperboard wrap with separate pen retention; quote plain packaging and branded packaging separately. Shipping mailer must protect corners and prevent ink contact.
  • Request itemized notebook unit costs at 25, 50, 100, and 300 notebooks per color, and matching pen unit costs and bundled kitting costs at the same quantities. Include MOQ per color, proof/setup charges, packaging, freight terms, and defect remedy.
  • Request traceable material declarations and certification evidence only for claims you intend to make. Do not label leather, recycled, archival, FSC, or eco-friendly without applicable evidence.

MOQ & quotation questions

  • Are you the actual factory or a trading company, and who manufactures the paper and binding?
  • What is the MOQ for each cover color, paper color, and printed grid? Can colors share a combined order?
  • For quantities of 25, 50, 100, and 300, quote itemized per-unit prices for notebook only, pen only, and notebook + pen bundle. Show color-specific MOQs, packaging, setup/proof, sample, and tooling charges separately.
  • Can you provide dyed-through paper, rather than a dark surface coating that may scratch?
  • Which paper weights accept opaque white gel ink? Can you send unbound sheets before making books?
  • What binding achieves the opening we want at this page count, and what tradeoffs should we expect?
  • What are the costs and lead times for swatches, blank samples, printed samples, and a signed production sample?
  • How are production colors matched to approved physical masters, and what variation do you accept?
  • Can we source the pen separately and have you pack it? What incoming pen checks can you perform?
  • Who owns the artwork and tooling? Is any tooling fee reusable on future orders?
  • What does the quoted price include, which currency and Incoterm applies, and how long is the quote valid?
  • How do you inspect binding, print registration, paper count, stains, damaged corners, and pens?
  • May we arrange an independent pre-shipment inspection? How are failed goods repaired or replaced?
  • What deposit is required, when is the balance due, and what happens if the sample or inspection fails?
  • What are carton dimensions, gross weights, shipping options, and documents for our intended destination?
  • Which claims can you document for this exact material and order? Are the certificates current and applicable?
  • What are your realistic production and transit ranges after sample approval, including peak-season delays?
  • What shipping methods to the United States can you quote? For each, state origin, Incoterm, transit estimate, shipping and insurance charges, duties/tax responsibilities, carton dimensions/weight, and tracking. These are questions; no shipping rate is verified.
  • What is the sample production time, production lead time after signed sample approval, and estimated transit time to the US? What could delay each stage?
  • Which written QC checks, tolerances, defect classes, pre-shipment inspection options, and rework/replacement terms can you offer? Provide records for the quoted order.

Sample quality checks

  • Write the same sentence and line grid with each candidate pen on all three papers. Photograph one-pass opacity and compare with two passes.
  • Perform fingertip smear checks after 5, 15, 30, and 60 seconds under recorded conditions. Proposed gate: legible, consistent writing with no visible transfer after 30 seconds in normal use; revise with users before production.
  • Test closed-page transfer after one minute, next-day transfer, back-of-page visibility, skipping after capped storage, and restart after a week.
  • Ask at least five users, including two left-handed writers, to write and sketch for 15 minutes; record hand transfer, readability, and friction.
  • Open each binding through 100 normal cycles; inspect hinge cracking, loose leaves, ribbon attachment, and how easily it stays open. This is an internal screening test, not a formal certification.
  • Carry a sample in a bag for a week; compare scuffing and corner wear. Test packed samples with ordinary courier handling before finalizing packaging.
  • Retain a signed ‘golden sample’ with approved paper, pen, cover, grid, packaging, and defect acceptance criteria before placing inventory.

Preorder strategy

  • Stage one is a free waitlist with no reservation or implied supply guarantee. Learn about use cases, colors, and price objections before requesting payment.
  • Do not accept paid preorders until a full production-equivalent sample is approved, ink and paper pass testing, the factory confirms capacity and MOQ, and freight/fulfillment costs are written down.
  • Choose one initial shipping region, provisionally the contiguous US for modeling. Validate that region with the founder and interviews; do not imply worldwide shipping.
  • Write an explicit preorder product page: price/currency, contents, approved specifications, payment timing, estimated ship window, tax/shipping treatment, cancellations/refunds, support contact, and delay-update process. Review it for the markets you will serve before taking money.
  • Prefer an in-stock pilot if cash allows. If using preorders, cap orders to documented capacity, include a realistic buffer in the delivery window, and retain enough accessible cash for refunds. A waitlist count is not working capital.
  • Invite the earliest confirmed subscribers first without inventing a sellout, deadline, or limited allocation. Explain any real cap and its reason.
  • Send status updates at the promised cadence, including when nothing has changed. Do not launch on day 30 merely because a calendar ends.

6. Practical launch plan

What you can do today

  • Review the working storefront and the launch kit; approve Veleto spelling, collection names, voice, colors, and the updated notebook, pen, and bundle price hypotheses.
  • Make a shortlist of ten people with a real notebook habit and invite five research conversations personally.
  • Use the supplied RFQ draft to compare three candidate manufacturers after checking who they are. No supplier has been contacted by this project.
  • Set a validation spending ceiling; start with a small sample scope instead of committing to inventory.
  • Choose the first delivery region and a sender/support address, then select the email service and hosting route when ready to publish.

What needs a supplier

  • Achievable dyed-through paper colors and weights, MOQ per color, binding method, grid production, cover composition, itemized quote, freight terms, lead times, and defect remedies.
  • A compatible pen with reliable replenishment, actual barrel appearance, testable ink, and permission for any proposed branding.
  • Manufacturable packaging dimensions and dielines; the supplied SVG is only a visual concept.

What needs physical samples

  • Paper feel and white-ink opacity, dry time, left-handed smudging, closed-page transfer, and pen restart reliability.
  • Binding behavior, actual color matching, durability, ribbon attachment, page count, and package protection.
  • True product photographs, sound recordings, creator sample content, and final claims.

Before significant spending

  • People understand that this is dark paper requiring a suitable pen, and see a reason to use it repeatedly.
  • The target price survives actual comparison with existing notebook habits; compliments and color votes alone are insufficient.
  • Enough demand exists for each color to justify its MOQ. Keep all three in the concept, but negotiate or adjust the initial batch if one color is weak.
  • Sample quality is repeatable, the landed cost is known, and contribution remains positive after fees, fulfillment, refunds, shipping, and acquisition.
  • The brand/domain can be used, the signup and support processes work, and the founder has approved policies for the actual selling markets.
